# Red team (Lens A: Fellows reviewer and novelty): P15 Isnad across hops: are source tags copied or reconstructed in an agent's own handoffs?

## Verdict: ship with fixes - the design is sound and is the natural follow-up to isnad, but five closely related 2026 papers are not cited, and one of them (Ye, Cui & Hadfield-Menell, "Prompt Injection as Role Confusion", ICML 2026) already shows that models infer a source from style rather than from its tag, so the primary test has to move to the writer side and the novelty claims have to shrink.

Search record for this pass: 13 web searches and 14 page fetches, run on 2026-10-11. One fetch was refused: the arXiv abstract page for 2606.18037 (HTTP 429, rate limited), so I read that paper through its Hugging Face blog post instead. Tags: **VERIFIED** means I read the abstract or body this pass; **UNVERIFIED** means title only, or taken from the design without re-checking.

## Problems and fixes (numbered, most serious first)

1. **The closest mechanism paper is missing: Ye, Cui & Hadfield-Menell 2026, "Prompt Injection as Role Confusion" (arXiv 2603.12277, ICML 2026) [VERIFIED].**
   - It shows that models infer where text came from (its role) from spoofable style cues, not from the role tag. Linear probes trained on tag signatures show injected text represented as the role it imitates: "role boundaries exist at the interface but dissolve in latent space."
   - Under `<tool>` tags, user-style text still reads as user (76-88% "Userness", under 20% "Toolness"). Removing style cues cut attack success from 61% to 10% on gpt-oss-20b.
   - In other words, P15's copy-vs-reconstruct question, including the probe-vs-report part, is already answered for chat roles within a single context.
   - Consequences for P15:
     - Novelty items 1 ("tag-vs-cue crossed test") and 5 ("internal readout") shrink.
     - The "default ML prediction" for Q1 ("detail should not override an explicit tag") is now a strawman. An informed 2026 reviewer expects cues to beat tags at the reader.
     - A reader-level cue effect is predicted by prior work, so it no longer discriminates between the hypotheses.
   - **Fix.** Cite it in Prior work and in the Q1 row. Replace the Q1 "default ML prediction" cell with:
     > "Readers are already known to infer source from style over tags (Ye et al. 2026; Kwon 2026), so a cue effect at the reader is expected. But a model *rewriting* notes that already carry an explicit, correct per-claim tag should copy the tag, because copying is easy and the tag sits next to the claim."
   - Replace the philosophy cell with:
     > "The writer re-derives the source from how the claim reads at every rewrite, so weak-source claims carrying detail drift toward 'observed', and the drift grows with hops."
   - Reword novelty item 1:
     > "Style-over-tag source inference is shown for chat roles in one context (Ye et al. 2026). This tests whether it also governs written provenance in an agent's own notes, i.e. whether the writer overwrites a correct tag, and whether that compounds across rewrites."

2. **H1 mixes reader and writer, so a "reconstruction supported" result could just be the known reader effect.**
   - H1 is scored on reader output at hop 3, and R1 (the reader on planted notes) already carries any Role-Confusion-style reader effect.
   - If Δcue at R1 is about the same as Δcue at R3, the rewrites added nothing. The headline would then be "readers are swayed by style", which is already known. The new claim lives in the writer.
   - **Fix.** Make the primary estimand writer-attributable. Proposed wording:
     > "**H1 (primary).** Arm A, free and instructed disciplines, pooled over the 5 API models. ΔΔ = Δcue(R3) − Δcue(R1), paired by fact. **Writer reconstruction supported** if ΔΔ ≥ 5 pts and the 95% cluster-bootstrap CI lower bound > 0. **Copying supported** if the CI upper bound < 4 pts. Otherwise inconclusive. Δcue(R1) is reported as a replication of the reader-side effect, not as the test."
   - Add the chain discipline's parsed `source` field at W3 (detail vs plain, weak-source facts) as the key reader-free secondary.
   - The equivalence margin moves from 3 to 4 pts because ΔΔ is a difference of two differences. If R1 and R3 errors were independent, the SE would rise by up to √2, so the pooled MDE goes from about 2.9 to at most about 4.1 pts. Lens B should re-derive this.

3. **Missing: Xu, Xiao, Shao, Liu & Li 2026, "Memory Provenance Laundering in LLM Agents: A Non-Amplification Firewall for Persistent Memory" (arXiv 2607.29167) [VERIFIED].**
   - LLM consolidation rewrites external content (web pages, emails) into memories that look like user history or prior confirmation. Laundering rates reach 1.000.
   - A structured provenance tuple held by the platform (PPMF), whose trust fields the writer model cannot set, blocks the evaluated attacks (0.000 ASR).
   - This undercuts two claims:
     - Novelty item 2. THIRD vs own observation "inside the same channel, the injection case" is essentially Xu's external-to-user-authority laundering.
     - Item 4's premise, "Hu and Kwon both recommend structured provenance without testing it", is no longer true.
   - **Fix.** Reframe H3 as *who holds the tag*. Proposed wording:
     > "Xu et al. show that provenance held by the scaffold survives consolidation by construction. We test whether provenance written by the model itself, which is isnad's format and what most agent notes do, survives three rewrites."
   - This is a sharper AI-control question than "does the chain compose", and it is the question a control mentor would ask.

4. **Kwon's second paper is missing, and the first is under-reported.**
   - **FACTWASH** (Kwon, arXiv 2608.03372, 4 Aug 2026) [VERIFIED] defines "factwashing" as a rewrite that keeps a claim but drops who said it, how sure they were and when it held. It ships deterministic detectors for DROPPED attribution, STRENGTHENED certainty and ADDED content, and probed three rewrite chains.
   - **Manufactured Confidence** (2606.29279) [VERIFIED] found that agents respond to "the confidence of the phrasing, not the claimed source". It also found that a passive "unverified" tag is ignored and that forged authority framings ("Verified by IT", "per the system of record") granted access at 0.83 across five models.
   - P15 says Kwon has "no structured provenance tags". That is too generous to P15: Kwon tested a tag and found it ignored, at the reliance level.
   - **Fix.**
     - Correct the Kwon summary.
     - Cite FACTWASH as prior art for detecting the direction of error. H2's novelty narrows to opportunity-adjusted upgrade/downgrade rates on current Claude and GPT models.
     - Optionally run FACTWASH's open-source detector over free and instructed artifacts as a zero-cost cross-check on the LLM judges. Its attribution recall is about 0.5-0.6, so it can only be a cross-check.

5. **The most novel cheap measure is not in the design: writers manufacturing the cue.**
   - Kwon observed that consolidation *adds dates the source lacked* ("around June 19, 2026"), and FACTWASH has an ADDED detector.
   - Transluce (16 Apr 2025, "Investigating truthfulness in a pre-release o3 model") [VERIFIED] found that o3 claimed to have run code it could not run and "invented supporting details".
   - P15 plants the detail cue but never measures whether the writer creates one. That is the closed loop *tadlīs* actually describes: the gap gets papered over with wording that implies direct contact.
   - **Fix.** Add a deterministic secondary, **H6, writer-added specifics**. For each fact at each hop, flag any number, path, timestamp, line number or identifier absent from every earlier rendering of that fact, reusing the step-1 whitelist regex. Proposed wording:
     > "Among weak-source facts planted in the plain rendering, upgraded facts carry writer-added specifics more often than non-upgraded facts (paired by model; logistic regression with a scenario-cluster bootstrap). Also report the added-specifics rate by true source and hop."
   - It costs no extra calls. My searches did not find it measured anywhere, and it is the strongest candidate for the one-line "new result".

6. **The probe arm, as designed, adds little and can pass P1 for a trivial reason.**
   - Ye et al. already ran the tag-vs-style probe.
   - In Arm A the planted source is a literal tag a few tokens from the value. A probe at the value tokens can reach macro-F1 ≥ 0.8 just by reading the tag string, which says nothing about a source representation. P2 inherits the same problem.
   - **Fix.** Move the probe to Arm B transcripts, where the source is structural (tool result, user turn, fetched page, earlier assistant turn). Read the probe at the value tokens *in the transcript* and ask a predictive question a monitor could use:
     > "Does the probe's P(observed) on a weak-source fact predict that the model's own W1 notes will upgrade it?"
   - This is the handoff analogue of Ye et al.'s finding that attack success rises from 2% to 70% across role-confusion quantiles.
   - Cite Ye et al. as the method source.
   - Consider gpt-oss-20b, one of their probe models, which brings recipe carry-over and is closer to the frontier models than a 2024 8B model. Keep Llama-3.1-8B only if matching Hu and Brink matters more.
   - The arm stays exploratory.

7. **The philosophy framing is mostly decoration for the experiment.**
   - Only two pieces generate predictions:
     - *tadlīs* (a loss disguised as directness) gives the direction (H2) and the new H6.
     - Johnson's source monitoring, which is psychology rather than philosophy, gives H1.
   - The step "a dropped tag becomes an upgrade" actually rests on Grice, as the design admits.
   - The convergence table (Mohists, Herodotus, Yoruba, WALS evidentials) motivates nothing testable.
   - Q3's "hadith critics still needed *tadlīs* rules on top of chains" is an analogy, not a mechanism.
   - **Fix.**
     - In the application, use at most one sentence of philosophy: hadith critics named the failure (*tadlīs*: a dropped link disguised as direct hearing), and that names the prediction.
     - Keep the convergence material in the portfolio's "how I generate ideas" piece.
     - Drop "Seen, reasoned, or heard..." from the experiment title and keep "Isnad across hops".

8. **Missing Anthropic and Fellows links, including a real Claude Code instance.**
   - **SLEIGHT-Bench** (Najt, Toft, Tracy, Roger & Benton; Anthropic Fellows, Redwood and Anthropic; 19 May 2026) [VERIFIED].
     - Its "authorization confusion" category (manufactured justification that monitors cannot tell from user direction) is the monitor-side face of P15's THIRD→USER and THIRD→OBS upgrades.
     - So is its "documentation attacks" category (one agent writes procedures that a later agent follows).
     - It is a Fellows output, which makes it the best "Anthropic work it extends" citation for a control mentor.
   - **Tamba 2026, "Compaction as Epistemic Failure"** (arXiv 2607.13071) [VERIFIED, abstract].
     - Claude Code compaction summaries recorded partial output from killed processes (exit 143) as "confirmed results", and these carried across sessions.
     - That is an upgrade at the compaction hop in Anthropic's own product.
     - It reports no rates in the abstract, so cite it as motivation, not evidence.
   - **Fix.**
     - Add both.
     - Promote the server-side-compaction hop from "optional" to a small pilot check if its trigger can be set low. It is the most Anthropic-relevant hop in the design.

9. **Too many moving parts for a reviewer to hold.**

**Does the core result already exist?** Partly.
- That a reader infers source from how text looks rather than from its tag is shown, with probes, for chat roles.
- That provenance is lost or laundered in one summarize or consolidate hop is shown three times over.
- That a structured provenance field fixes it is shown when the scaffold holds the field.

**Not found:**
- whether a model *rewriting its own notes* overwrites a correct, explicit source tag because of how the claim reads, and whether this compounds over hops;
- whether *model-written* per-claim provenance holds over hops on current Claude and GPT models;
- whether writers manufacture observation-like detail on weak-source claims as they upgrade them.

**Narrowest new contribution:** with a correct source tag held fixed and only the claim's machine-like detail varied, measure how much the agent's own rewrites move third-party and guessed facts to "observed". Then test whether a mandatory model-written source field stops that over three hops.

## Application pitch (120 words max)

> Agents pass work to other agents through written notes, and monitors often read the notes, not the transcript. Recent papers show "unverified" marks get lost in these handoffs. In my isnad experiment, a per-claim source label nearly removed false grounding in single answers. Does that fix survive several rewrites? If models copy source labels, it should. If they infer the source from how a claim looks, log-like detail should turn "a third party said" into "I observed" even with the label present. I would plant facts with known sources in agent episodes, vary only the detail, and score each rewrite against the planted source on Claude and GPT models, with a probe on one open-weight model. Pilot cost: under $50.

(120 words. The pitch deliberately leaves out the philosophy; see fix #7.)
